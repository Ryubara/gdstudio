# Deadnight's design philosophy

<!-- new section: games/deadnight/ held only the performance review; the summary pages join it with gdstudio #770 -->

Three pillars decide what goes into Deadnight and what does not (Deadnight's
[Design](https://ryubara.github.io/deadnight/design/)):

1. **Light is survival.** The generator powers the lot's lights and the projector, and the dead are faster off lit
   ground. Points buy fuel, doors and weapons, so every round asks what stays powered.
2. **A night that closes in.** Rounds grow harder and the night darker; the map opens outward from the screen to the
   ticket plaza at the back of the lot.
3. **Played together.** Built for two to eight people in a browser or a Discord call, with bots on top; solo is
   supported, never the focus.

## What follows from them {#choices}

- **A night starts equal.** Every player starts a night with the same points, fuel and lot; what carries over between
  nights is a level, cosmetics, challenges and only a slightly better kit, and nothing is sold
  ([Permanent progression](https://ryubara.github.io/deadnight/design/progression/)).
- **Read at a glance, on a phone.** The game is played on a phone held sideways as much as on a desktop: the HUD, the
  prompts and the controls are judged at the narrowest phone, by captures, not by a desktop window.
- **Original, studied from the genre.** Round-based zombie games are studied for their design and used as visual
  reference; Deadnight's content, names, layouts and assets are its own.
- **Measured, not guessed.** A balance number, a crowd cap or a frame budget comes from a run through the real game
  (the balance simulator, the bots, the benchmarks), written on a research page with its numbers.
