# Prototype mannequins

<!-- new section: GDStudio's own prototype bodies (kit #1149) are generated, not cleaned from a kit; no page covered
what a prototype body is for, how one rig serves many proportions, or how these are built and measured -->

GDStudio's own prototype bodies: a male, a female and a chibi body, each in three looks, generated by script and rigged
on one skeleton ([The prototype mannequin rig](mannequin-rig.md)). They live in the 3D Third Person template under
`assets/characters/prototype/`. The maintainer chose the `form` look (kit #1172): `form_female` is the template's
default body and `form_male` the enemies' since kit #1187 ([the bodies](../../engines/godot/third-person/character.md#mannequins)).
The Quaternius mannequins stay beside them.

## What a prototype body is for {#purpose}

<!-- new section: the research the design rests on, with its sources -->

A prototype body exists to judge motion and scale before any art is made: it must read at a distance, show which way
it faces and which limb is which, carry no style of its own, cost little, and take any clip. Unreal's prototype
mannequins are the reference for the idea (studied, not copied): a neutral body on a skeleton that other characters
share. What we took from the research, and where each fact is from:

| Question | What we found | What we did |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bone count and names | The Universal Animation Library's rig has 65 bones in Unreal-style names ([measured on the file](../../engines/godot/third-person/character.md#mannequins)); Unreal's own mannequins are reported at 68 (UE4) and 89 (UE5) bones, MetaHuman at 342 ([Reallusion's manual](https://manual.reallusion.com/Metahuman-Interoperability/Content/MetaHuman-Interoperability/17-Metahuman-Interoperability/Skeleton-between-CC-and-UE-Characters.htm), not Epic's). Godot's humanoid profile has 56 ([SkeletonProfileHumanoid](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_skeletonprofilehumanoid.html)), VRM's 55 with 15 required ([VRM humanoid](https://github.com/vrm-c/vrm-specification/blob/master/specification/VRMC_vrm-1.0/humanoid.md)). VRChat ranks a mobile avatar "excellent" at 75 bones and "good" at 90 ([performance ranks](https://creators.vrchat.com/avatars/avatar-performance-ranking-system/)). | 82 bones: the library's 65 to the letter and 17 leaves. 58 carry skin. |
| One skeleton, many bodies | Unreal shares a skeleton across bodies by retargeting only translations: every bone takes its translation from the body ("Skeleton"), the pelvis scales the clip's by the body ("AnimationScaled"), the root and the IK and weapon bones keep the clip's ("Animation"); rotation always comes from the clip, and for end effectors "there is no automatic work happening" ([Animation Retargeting](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/animation-retargeting-in-unreal-engine)). Bodies that differ a lot go through the IK Retargeter, with IK goals for hands and feet ([IK Rig retargeting](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/ik-rig-animation-retargeting-in-unreal-engine)). | The same scheme in Godot: bodies differ in rest translations only, the pelvis's keys scale by `Skeleton3D.motion_scale`, and the `ik_` bones are there for the contacts. |
| Godot's own retarget | The importer's retarget maps a rig onto the humanoid profile and can rewrite rests; its "Overwrite Axis" "can produce horrible results if the original Bone Rest set externally is important", and sharing motion across heights "may cause the appearance of slipping" ([Retargeting 3D Skeletons](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/retargeting_3d_skeletons.html)). `motion_scale` "multiplies the 3D position track animation" ([Skeleton3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_skeleton3d.html)). | No import retarget: the rig already is the clips' rig. Only `motion_scale` is used, set by the import script. |
| T or A rest | VRM requires a T pose ([VRM T pose](https://github.com/vrm-c/vrm-specification/blob/master/specification/VRMC_vrm-1.0/tpose.md)); Godot's retarget wants "Fix Silhouette" for A-posed models; Unreal needs a retarget pose between a T and an A source. A T rest is blamed for poor shoulders once the arm hangs. | T, because the library's rest is a T and clips store rotations in the parent. The shoulder and hip are balls in sockets, so nothing is blended there ([the rest pose](mannequin-rig.md#rest-pose)). |
| Twist bones | Linear blend skinning wrings a limb thin where it rolls (the candy wrapper); the cure is a bone that takes a share of the roll, driven and never keyed. Godot 4.6 added `BoneTwistDisperser3D`, which spreads an end bone's twist over its parents in a chain ([class reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_bonetwistdisperser3d.html), [the 4.6 IK article](https://godotengine.org/article/inverse-kinematics-returns-to-godot-4-6/)). Godot has no dual quaternion skinning in the stock renderer (only a community demo was found). | Three leaf twist bones a side, turned by `MannequinTwist`, a 60-line `SkeletonModifier3D`. The engine's disperser works on bones in the chain, which would change the library's hierarchy. |
| Sockets and IK bones | Unreal's mannequin carries `ik_foot`, `ik_hand` and `ik_hand_gun` bones that do nothing by themselves and are targets for two-bone IK ([forum explanation](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/explanation-of-the-mannequin-ik-bones/45166)); Godot attaches by `BoneAttachment3D` ([class reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_boneattachment3d.html)). | Seven socket bones and four IK targets, by name on every body. |
| Root motion | Godot's `root_motion_track` takes the track out of the pose and hands the motion to the game ([AnimationMixer](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_animationmixer.html)); the library ships in-place and root-motion versions ([its page](https://quaternius.itch.io/universal-animation-library)). | A root bone on the floor; clips in place, the game moves the character. |
| Face and fingers | Humanoid profiles keep three joints a finger (Godot and VRM add a thumb metacarpal) and at most a jaw and eyes. | Three joints a finger as the library has them; no face bones: a visor shows where the head looks. |
| Levels of detail | Unreal removes bones per level by a list ([Skeletal Mesh LODs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/skeletal-mesh-lods-in-unreal-engine)); Godot's automatic mesh LOD "may occasionally introduce rendering issues (especially in skinned meshes)" ([Mesh LOD](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/mesh_lod.html)). | Three levels made by the generator with fewer sides and rings, shown by visibility ranges; the far level drops the finger and twist bones from its skin (28 or 19 bones). Automatic LOD is off. |
| Web cost | Four bone influences are the compatible choice ("All Influences ... may not be compatible with all renderers": [import configuration](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/import_configuration.html)); Godot's importer refuses Draco. VRChat's mobile ranks: 7,500 triangles "excellent", 10,000 "good". | At most four influences (the lay figure one), no compression extension, one material, one 32 px palette. |

Where the scheme is known to break, and what the measurements below show of it: rotations carry over but contacts do
not. A body with shorter arms does not bring its hands together where the clip's body did, and a body with thicker
limbs rests them inside each other (the chibi's forearm goes 4 to 5 cm into its thigh in `Sitting_Idle`). The feet
stay planted only while the leg keeps the reference's proportions. The answer in every engine studied is IK on the
contacts, which is what the `ik_` bones are for; no clip uses them yet (kit #1150 plans the clips).

## The bodies and the looks {#bodies-and-looks}

<!-- new section: what was built, with its numbers -->

A body is numbers (`bodies.py`: joint places and girths), a look is how they are drawn (`styles.py`), and the skin is
tubes lofted along the bones with weights worked out from where each ring is along its bone (`shapes.py`): nothing is
sculpted or painted, so a game makes its own body by copying a body's numbers and building.

| Body | Height | Hips (`motion_scale`) | What differs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `male` | 1.80 m | 0.917 m (1.0) | the reference legs and trunk; shoulders 0.19 m out, arm 0.28 + 0.265 m, hand 0.195 m |
| `female` | 1.745 m | 0.917 m (1.0) | the same legs, a trunk 4 cm shorter, shoulders 0.16 m out, arm 0.262 + 0.248 m, hand 0.181 m, a narrower waist and wider hips |
| `chibi` | 1.25 m | 0.500 m (0.545) | the reference leg at 0.545, a head a third of the height, arm 0.155 + 0.15 m, hand 0.172 m with thick fingers (stylised: 13.7 % of its height) |

| Look | What it is | Triangles at levels 0, 1, 2 | Skin bones | File, gzipped |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `form` | one bending skin a limb, dark bands at the joints, a twist stripe down each limb; uses the twist bones | 4,120 / 1,936 / 1,008 | 58 / 58 / 28 | 322 KB, 84 KB |
| `joint` | rigid shells on dark ball joints (a lay figure): one bone a vertex, nothing stretches | 7,760 / 3,800 / 2,168 | 49 / 49 / 19 | 517 KB, 124 KB |
| `facet` | six-sided limbs, flat shaded, heavy forearms and calves: sits beside low-poly art | 1,928 / 1,392 / 616 | 58 / 58 / 28 | 483 KB, 71 KB |

**Hands are a person's size, not the library rig's.** The reference rig's hand is 0.232 m from the wrist to the
middle fingertip, 12.9 % of a 1.80 m body; a person's is 10 to 11 % of the height and about three quarters of the
forearm (the usual anthropometric figures: a woman's hand about 0.17 to 0.18 m long and 74 to 79 mm across the palm,
a man's about 0.19 m and 84 to 90 mm; not re-sourced for this page). The finger bones keep the reference hand's layout
at one scale a body:

| Body | Hand length | Share of height | Hand over forearm | Palm width |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `male` | 0.195 m (scale 0.84) | 10.8 % | 0.74 | 84 mm |
| `female` | 0.181 m (scale 0.78) | 10.4 % | 0.73 | 74 mm |
| `chibi` | 0.172 m (scale 0.74) | 13.7 % | 1.15 | 100 mm (stylised on purpose) |

For comparison the Quaternius male is 13,744 triangles in 618 KB (191 KB gzipped) and the female 14,612 in 1,443 KB
(300 KB). A file holds all three levels. Flat shading costs vertices: `facet` has half the triangles of `form` and
half again as many vertices (4,033 against 2,714 at level 0), because a flat face cannot share a vertex.

Every look marks what a prototype needs to read: a dark visor (where the head looks), a mark on the chest (front from
back), a stripe down the outside of each limb in one colour on the left and another on the right (which limb, and how
far it has rolled), lighter palms and dark soles (the hand's and the foot's turn). The colours are nine cells of a
32 px palette embedded in each file.

- **Approach:** Build a body from lofted tubes with weights computed from each ring's place along its bone, and let
  the shoulder and hip be a dome that turns inside the trunk's shell. **Watch out for:** a foot whose sole is flat to
  its ends dug its heel and toes 18 to 31 mm under the floor where the reference mannequins go 7 to 12 (`Crouch_Fwd`,
  `Slide`); raising the heel's and the toes' last ring by 6 to 7 % of the ankle's height and shortening the heel
  brought every clip within 10 mm of the reference. **Seen:** gdstudio #1149, 2026-11-11.
- **Approach:** Keep the leg's proportions (thigh, calf, ankle height) when a body changes size, and scale the whole
  leg by one number; change anything else freely. **Watch out for:** the pelvis's keys scale by one number
  (`motion_scale`), so a leg with another thigh-to-calf ratio reaches the floor at another knee angle than the clip
  keys; and the game must move a scaled body at the clip's speed times `motion_scale`, or the feet slide.
  **Seen:** gdstudio #1149, 2026-11-11 (the chibi's leg is the reference's at 0.545; its feet read the reference's
  heights times 0.545 to 7 mm).
- **Approach:** Measure a body's hands before review: length over height (10 to 11 %), length over the forearm
  (about 0.75) and the palm's width, for each sex. **Watch out for:** the female's hand was the library rig's at
  0.92, 0.214 m, 12.2 % of her height and 0.86 of her forearm, and the male's 12.9 %: a kit mannequin's hand is
  larger than a person's. **Seen:** gdstudio #1149, 2026-11-11, after the maintainer's review of #1151 found a
  woman's hands far too large (fix: scales 0.78 and 0.84, the table above).
- **Approach:** Measure a finger pose or curl made on the library's hand again on each `form` hand before it plays
  there. **Watch out for:** the `form` hands rest their fingers and thumb differently from the Quaternius hand, so a
  curl keyed against one does not close the same way on the other. **Seen:** gdstudio #1155 (PR #1275), 2026-11-12.

## Proof in Godot {#proof}

<!-- new section: how a body is proved to play the libraries, and the numbers of these bodies -->

`tests/assets/test_prototype_mannequins.gd` holds the limits; `tools/mannequin/measure_bodies.gd` prints the table for
eighteen clips; `tests/fixtures/body_measure.gd` is the measure (the skin worked out on the processor from Godot's own
pose, limbs as capsules with the radius the body's skin has at rest, read beyond what the same two limbs overlap at
rest). The Quaternius mannequins are measured in the same run, and a body is read against them.

- **Tracks:** every track of every clip of both libraries finds its bone on all nine bodies (0 missing).
- **Feet:** over eighteen clips the lowest foot vertex of every body is within 10 mm of the reference mannequins'
  (times `motion_scale`); the worst is `Slide`. The library itself dips the toes 20 to 26 mm in `Walk`.
- **Limbs:** the adult bodies overlap at most 15 mm more than the reference in any clip (`facet_female`, forearm on
  thigh in `Sitting_Idle`); `form_male` and both `joint` adults never more. The chibi goes 40 to 53 mm deeper in
  `Sitting_Idle` and 13 to 18 mm in the crouch (chest on thigh): the known limit above.
- **Hands:** the measure's capsules end at the wrist and its feet are the foot's vertices, so it does not see a
  hand: resizing the hands left every number of the table as it was, to 0.1 mm (rerun on the resized bodies). A
  hand's contact is judged by eye, in the close view of `tools/capture/mannequin_parade.tscn` (a hand roll, the
  pistol clips and a reach).
- **Cost:** forty characters playing `Walk`, clips and bones and modifiers advanced by hand, headless: 17 to 21
  microseconds a character a frame for every body and for the Quaternius male alike; the 17 added bones and the twist
  modifier are inside the 2 microseconds the same run varies by (`tools/mannequin/body_cost.gd`). What a browser
  pays to draw them is [below](#browser-cost).

- **Approach:** Give the levels of detail visibility ranges that meet with no margin, and look at the body in a real
  render at the distance the game's camera stands. **Watch out for:** with a begin and end margin of 0.75 m, bodies
  standing 8.7 m from the camera (inside the band between level 0's end and level 1's begin at 9 m) were drawn at
  neither level: the headless tests passed and the first Godot capture showed only the Quaternius mannequin.
  **Seen:** gdstudio #1149, 2026-11-11 (fix: no margins, and the test asserts it).
- **Approach:** Time a headless bench by the work itself: advance every `AnimationPlayer` and `Skeleton3D` by hand
  (`callback_mode_process` and `modifier_callback_mode_process` set to manual) between two reads of the clock.
  **Watch out for:** timing whole frames read 172.5 microseconds a character for every model to the decimal: a
  headless Godot waits between frames, so the number was the frame's length over the crowd's size. **Seen:** gdstudio
  #1149, 2026-11-11.
- **Approach:** Run a GdUnit suite or a `-s` script with a timeout and its output in a file, without `-d`, and read
  the file's size before its contents. **Watch out for:** with `-d` a script that does not parse stops in the
  debugger and prints the same line until the timeout (4.7 MB of log); a `-s` script that fails never quits.
  **Seen:** gdstudio #1149, 2026-11-11.
- **Approach:** Measure a body's contact with the floor or with itself on the mesh the camera sees up close
  (`CharacterBodies.near_meshes`); keep a green run's printed numbers by running the suite in a kept game
  (`gds template test --keep --suite ...`, then the pinned Godot headless on it with `-s
  res://addons/gdUnit4/bin/GdUnitCmdTool.gd -a <suite>` into a file); run the whole suite with nothing beside it.
  **Watch out for:** the farthest level's hand is a mitten whose thumb no clip moves, 31 to 36 mm under the floor
  wherever a flat hand rests (beyond 22 m: the far mesh, not a clip); `gds template test` does not print its
  log's path after every green run; run beside Blender renders and two captures, the suite failed two timing
  budgets (`test_net_server_cost.gd`, `test_parts_budget.gd`) that pass alone. **Seen:** gdstudio #1293,
  2026-11-11 and 2026-11-12.
- **Approach:** Do not count on `meshes/ensure_tangents=false` to shrink a skinned mesh: check the imported surface's
  format. **Watch out for:** the file holds no tangents and the option was off, and the imported mesh still carried
  a tangent array. **Seen:** gdstudio #1149, 2026-11-11 (left as it is; the test does not claim it).

### What a browser pays to draw them {#browser-cost}

<!-- new section: the proof above measures the processor's share headless; what the web build pays to skin and draw
the bodies (gdstudio #1186) has numbers and a bench of its own -->

`tools/bench/mannequin_draw_bench.tscn` stands 1, 8 or 16 males of one model playing `Walk` in place under the
game's sun (shadows on), at 4.5, 14 and 30 m from the camera (inside levels of detail 0, 1 and 2), and walks every
cell in turn in each round, so a busy minute hits every row; `mannequin_draw_bench.py` reads it in Chrome, recording
each `requestAnimationFrame` delta and each program WebGL links under the cell being measured. Make the build and run
it with `gds template bench-web 3d-third-person mannequin_draw_bench --run`, adding `--probe-args="--gpu --uncapped"`
for the GPU ([A bench's web export](../../engines/godot/web-probes.md#bench-web)); the page's query picks rounds,
frames and cells (the script's header). The numbers below: 640 x 360, uncapped, four GPU runs (an RTX 5080, 8
rounds of 60 frames) and two SwiftShader runs (6 rounds of 40), interleaved, on a workstation shared with about 25
agents. A row is the engine's mean frame of the median round; "a body" is (16 bodies minus none) / 16.

| Model, 16 bodies | GPU frame p50 / p95, near | GPU a body, level 0 / 1 / 2 | SwiftShader a body, level 0 / 1 / 2 | Draw calls a body |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Quaternius male | 2.6 to 3.0 / 8.2 to 10.9 ms | 0.12 to 0.15 / 0.12 to 0.15 / 0.12 to 0.15 ms | 2.5 to 2.7 / 1.4 to 1.5 / 0.95 to 1.0 ms | 4 |
| `form` | 2.1 to 2.2 / 4.4 to 6.2 ms | 0.09 to 0.10 / 0.08 to 0.10 / 0.09 to 0.10 ms | 2.8 to 2.9 / 1.2 / 0.64 to 0.68 ms | 2 |
| `joint` | 2.0 to 2.2 / 4.7 to 6.4 ms | 0.09 to 0.10 / 0.08 to 0.09 / 0.08 to 0.10 ms | **4.5** / **2.1 to 2.2** / **1.2** ms | 2 |
| `facet` | 2.1 to 2.3 / 4.8 to 7.4 ms | 0.09 to 0.10 / 0.08 to 0.10 / 0.08 to 0.10 ms | 1.6 to 1.7 / 1.0 / 0.50 to 0.53 ms | 2 |

The floor and sun alone are 0.6 to 0.7 ms on the GPU and 21 to 22 ms on SwiftShader.

- **On a GPU every look costs less than the Quaternius body**, about two thirds of it at every level. A prototype
  body is one surface with one material, so it is skinned once and drawn twice a frame (the view and the sun's
  shadow); the Quaternius male has two surfaces, so four draws. Split for 16 near bodies: drawing a body that does not
  move is 0.01 ms (0.02 for the Quaternius), the clip and skeleton alone (hidden) 0.03 ms (0.02: the 17 more bones and
  the twist modifier), and the rest of the 0.09 is skinning a moving body that is on screen. `MannequinTwist` is about
  0.01 ms a body near (16 bodies with it off: 0.08 to 0.09 ms each) and nothing past 9 m, where it skips.
- **On SwiftShader the cost follows the triangles drawn**, and a still body costs what a moving one does. `joint`
  (7,760 triangles at level 0, 2.2 times the Quaternius body's 3,480) costs 1.7 times the Quaternius body near and 1.3
  to 1.5 times beyond: it is the one look that costs more (follow-up #1259). `form` costs 5 to 10 % more near and less
  beyond; `facet` (1,928 triangles, its 4,033 vertices notwithstanding) costs least everywhere. The Quaternius body has
  no levels of its own; Godot's own mesh LODs (`meshes/generate_lods`) halve its triangles by 14 m.
- **The levels switch as built**: the primitives a frame match each level's triangles twice over (view and shadow)
  at every distance, for every look.
- **First appearance:** the first skinned body a page draws links 9 programs (74 to 80 ms on the GPU, 140 to 164 on
  SwiftShader); the first body of the other material 6 more (53 to 70 ms and 89 to 119), whichever comes first; the
  other two looks link nothing (3 ms on the GPU), since the three looks share one material's shader. A game whose
  player wears a prototype body builds them behind its loading screen; a second model in one scene (an AI in another
  body) pays its 6 programs where it first appears unless the warm-up draws it
  ([first-use hitches](../../engines/godot/first-use-hitches.md#the-warm-up)).
- **The slow far levels were a buffer hint (#1260).** In 5 page loads of 10 one look's far levels (1 and 2) read 0.2 to
  0.45 ms a body for some or all rounds. The engine skins each `MeshInstance3D` into a buffer of its own made with
  `GL_DYNAMIC_DRAW`, which ANGLE's Direct3D 11 copies out at every draw until the buffer has been drawn 3 times its
  size with no skinning in between ([the buffer hint](../../engines/godot/web-export.md#skin-buffer-hint)). The
  bench's `frozen` cells held every near body still, so the level 0 buffers (and the Quaternius body's one mesh) were
  bound directly from the first round on; a far level's own buffers only got there by chance, at a different round on
  each page, or never. Keeping each round's numbers in order showed it: every cell started slow and flipped once to
  its normal cost, and two other builds of the levels, one `MeshInstance3D` given each level's mesh (`@swap`) and
  Godot's own LODs on `Body_LOD0` (`@lods`, as the Quaternius body has), were never frozen and stayed slow (0.10 to
  0.15 ms a body against 0.06 to 0.07), so the three nodes under visibility ranges were not the cause.
  `SkinBuffers` (gds_game) makes those buffers static from the page. Ten page loads with it, on a GPU, uncapped, the
  full cell list with both other builds, on a workstation at 10 to 79 % processor load with about 30 agents:

  | 16 bodies, a body | With `SkinBuffers` (10 loads) | Without (`--dynamic-skin`, 4 loads between them) |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Prototype looks, far levels: median round / worst round | 0.05 to 0.09 / 0.06 to 0.12 ms | 0.06 to 0.11 / 0.09 to 0.18 ms |
  | Prototype looks, far, `@swap` and `@lods` | 0.05 to 0.09 / 0.06 to 0.12 ms | 0.09 to 0.15 / 0.11 to 0.21 ms |
  | Prototype looks, level 0 | 0.06 to 0.09 / 0.06 to 0.14 ms | 0.06 to 0.08 / 0.09 to 0.17 ms |
  | Quaternius male, far / near | 0.08 to 0.11 / 0.08 to 0.12 ms | 0.09 to 0.13 / 0.10 to 0.13 ms |

  No far cell's median was over 0.09 ms in any of the ten loads (1,041 buffers made static a page). A page with no
  frozen cell (`first=0`, the far cells only, the closest to a game, where no body holds still) read 0.10 ms a body
  for every look without it and 0.06 with it. The table above was measured before the fix, in the bound state the
  frozen cells gave; a game without `SkinBuffers` pays the streamed cost for every animated body.

- **Approach:** Under a shared machine's load, measure a browser bench in many short interleaved rounds (8 of 60
  frames, not 3 of 150) and read the median and the best round, never the mean of all. **Watch out for:** the first
  GPU run, 3 rounds, had one round in three 2 to 4 times slower for whole stretches of cells, and its medians said the
  prototype bodies cost twice the Quaternius body at levels 1 and 2; four runs of 8 rounds said two thirds. **Seen:**
  gdstudio #1186, 2026-11-11.

## Building and rendering {#building}

<!-- new section: how to rebuild, add a body and render the review sheets -->

```sh
blender -b --factory-startup -P build_mannequins.py                # all nine, into the folder; same bytes each run
blender -b --factory-startup -P build_mannequins.py -- --body chibi --look facet --out <folder>
blender -b --factory-startup -P render_mannequins.py -- --model <a.glb> --model <b.glb> --out <png> \
    --views front,quarter,side --pose relaxed          # or --mode wire|rig, --zoom hand, --clip <glb> --action Walk --frames 0,6,12
```

The build reads each file back before keeping it and fails on: a rest rotation of the 65 library bones that is not the
library's (1e-4), a bone count that is not 82, a level over its triangle budget (8,000, 4,000, 2,200), a lowest vertex
more than 0.6 mm off the floor, more than four bones a vertex, weights that do not sum to one, skin on a bone that
carries none, and a vertex with no mirror. It writes `rig_<body>.json` (the whole rest pose, for clip tools) and
`report.json`. To add a body, add its numbers to `bodies.py` and build; to use one in a game, point its `.glb`'s
import script at `mannequin_import.gd`.

- **Approach:** Render a review sheet one cell at a time (one model, one view, alone in the scene) and join the
  cells, and print the sheet's size before opening it. **Watch out for:** models laid out side by side in one scene
  overlapped their neighbours' cells in every close-up, and a close-up's layout made a 13,919 px wide image; a
  flat-shaded dark face showed grey on one side until the workbench's specular highlight was turned off.
  **Seen:** gdstudio #1149, 2026-11-11 (`render_mannequins.py`).
